# Deadeye vs the market, 2010-2026 (phase 3)

Generated by `python -m lab.archive_backtest` from `lab/data/history.db`. Every number is computed; nothing is estimated by hand. Intervals are 95% bootstrap intervals, by game and by whole season; a difference whose interval includes 0 is **not** evidence either way.

**What this rests on.** The Prediction Tracker's season archives (`nfl10.csv` ... `nfl26.csv`, downloaded by Sam on 2026-10-12). They are **undated**: the files do not say when each forecast was posted or whether it was revised. The line is the archive's *midweek* line (2010-2012 have none, so their latest line is used). The pool line is a **proxy** for the commissioner's (the next whole number above an opening spread of 6.5 or more), not his real lines. Deadeye is production's own `pricing.price`, unchanged; production counts the file's `linemidweek` column among the models, and so does this.

Games priced: **4223** with a result; **4215** graded under the proxy pool line (8 straight-up ties left out).

## Does the archive match what production saw?

Weeks with both: 2026 week 3 (16 games). Deadeye priced at 0.33 from each:

- the archive's midweek line equals the line production priced from on 14 of 16 games;
- models per game: production 44-44, archive 43-44;
- Deadeye's expected margin differs by 0.06 points on average (largest 0.36).

One week is all that can be checked, so how well the archive stands in for what Deadeye would have seen in earlier seasons is an assumption, not a measurement.
